2. **3s - 15s (GIẢI THÍCH):** "Everything in the frame — foreground, middle ground, and background — is in sharp focus simultaneously, allowing the viewer to explore the entire image and discover relationships between planes. Orson Welles and Gregg Toland made deep focus the defining visual innovation of "Citizen Kane" (1941), composing shots where action in the foreground, middle ground, and background all demanded simultaneous attention. William Wyler used deep focus in "The Best Years of Our Lives" to create some of cinema's most layered compositions. Jean Renoir's deep-focus staging in "Rules of the Game" lets multiple storylines play out in a single frame. The technique gives audiences agency — André Bazin argued it was more democratic than montage."
