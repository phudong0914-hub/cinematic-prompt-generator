2. **3s - 15s (GIẢI THÍCH):** "Also called a dolly zoom — the camera dollies in while zooming out (or vice versa), causing the background to warp while the subject stays the same size, creating a visceral sense of disorientation. Invented by cameraman Irmin Roberts for Alfred Hitchcock's "Vertigo" (1958) to visualize James Stewart's acrophobia, the technique was later used to devastating effect by Steven Spielberg in "Jaws" — the moment Chief Brody sees the shark attack from the beach. Peter Jackson employed it in "The Lord of the Rings" when Frodo senses the Ringwraiths approaching, and Sam Raimi made it a horror staple in the "Evil Dead" films."
