2. **3s - 15s (GIẢI THÍCH):** "A shot taken from directly overhead, looking straight down on the subject, creating a god-like perspective that can make subjects appear small and insignificant or reveal patterns invisible from ground level. Busby Berkeley pioneered the technique in 1930s musicals, choreographing dancers into kaleidoscopic geometric formations seen from directly above. Darren Aronofsky used it extensively in "Requiem for a Dream" to convey psychological detachment, and Wes Anderson frequently employs overhead shots of meticulously arranged objects as a signature compositional device."
