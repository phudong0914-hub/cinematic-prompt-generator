2. **3s - 15s (GIẢI THÍCH):** "A 1960s movement that broke every rule — jump cuts, handheld cameras, location shooting, fourth-wall breaks, and a rebellious rejection of polished studio filmmaking, treating cinema as conversation. Jean-Luc Godard's "Breathless" (1960) and François Truffaut's "The 400 Blows" (1959) launched the movement, joined by Agnès Varda, Jacques Rivette, Eric Rohmer, and Claude Chabrol. Cinematographer Raoul Coutard defined the visual style — handheld 16mm, natural light, real Parisian locations. The movement's influence is incalculable: Scorsese, Tarantino, Wes Anderson, Sofia Coppola, and Noah Baumbach all trace their artistic lineage directly to the Nouvelle Vague."
