2. **3s - 15s (GIẢI THÍCH):** "Frames the subject from roughly mid-thigh up, named after Western films where the frame needed to include a gunslinger's holstered weapon, conveying casual authority. Sergio Leone codified this framing in his Dollars trilogy, making the cowboy shot synonymous with Clint Eastwood's laconic gunfighter stance. Tarantino pays homage to the cowboy shot throughout "Kill Bill" and "Django Unchained," and it has migrated beyond Westerns — John Woo uses the same mid-thigh framing for his dual-pistol action heroes in "Hard Boiled" and "The Killer.""
