Robert Wiene's "The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari" (1920) established the movement with painted shadows and impossible architecture. F.W. Murnau's "Nosferatu" and Fritz Lang's "Metropolis" expanded the vocabulary. When these filmmakers fled Nazi Germany, they brought Expressionism to Hollywood, directly influencing film noir. Tim Burton's "Batman," "Edward Scissorhands," and "Batman Returns" are modern Expressionism, and Guillermo del Toro's production design carries the movement's DNA."
